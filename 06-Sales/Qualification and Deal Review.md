# Qualification and Deal Review — التأهيل ومراجعة الصفقات

## For future Claude

How Code Square decides **which deals to work and which to walk away from**, and how it reviews the ones in flight. `proposed`, 2026-09-02.

The qualification framework below is **MEDDPICC adapted**, not pasted. MEDDPICC was built for enterprise software sales with procurement committees and six-month cycles; applied unmodified to a Port Said shipping agency with eleven employees it produces theatre, not judgement. Each element is renamed in plain language, and two elements are dropped as inapplicable at this size. What each one means is explained for a reader with no sales training.

No invented prices (`[[Pricing and Packaging]]`), no invented clients, no invented team members (owner `TBD`).

---

## Part 1 — The framework: و.ق.ن.ق.ن.ص

Six things you must know before a deal is real. The Arabic initials spell nothing memorable, so use the numbers.

| # | Element | Arabic | The plain question | MEDDPICC origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The number** | الرقم | What is this costing them, in money or hours, right now? | Metrics |
| 2 | **Who pays** | مين بيدفع | Who physically approves the money — and have we met them? | Economic Buyer |
| 3 | **How they'll choose** | هيقرروا إزاي | What are they comparing us against, and on what basis? | Decision Criteria + Competition |
| 4 | **The steps to yes** | خطوات القرار | What literally has to happen between now and a signature? | Decision Process + Paper Process |
| 5 | **The pain** | الوجع | What breaks if they do nothing? | Identify Pain |
| 6 | **Our person inside** | نصيرنا | Who inside wants this and will argue for it when we're not there? | Champion |

**Dropped deliberately:** *Paper Process* is folded into element 4 (an SMB has no procurement department — the paper process is "the owner signs"). *Competition* is folded into element 3 (at this size the competitor is usually a freelancer, a template, or doing nothing, not another vendor in a bake-off).

---

### 1 · الرقم — The number

**What it means:** a figure that says what the current situation costs. Hours per week, errors per month, lost jobs, money.

**Why it decides deals:** without a number, price has nothing to be compared against, and every quote sounds expensive. With a number, the conversation stops being "is this expensive" and becomes "is this cheaper than what I'm already losing".

**How you get it:** Block B of `[[Discovery Call Script]]`.

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | We only have adjectives — "بياخد وقت", "بيتعب" |
| ⚠️ Weak | We estimated a number; they never confirmed it |
| ✅ Strong | **They** said the number, out loud, and it is in the notes in their words |

---

### 2 · مين بيدفع — Who pays

**What it means:** the person who can approve the spend without asking anyone else.

**Why it decides deals:** in an Egyptian family business this is almost always the owner, and often not the person you have been talking to. Deals die here more than anywhere else — pleasantly, silently, and late.

**How you get it:** ask directly (Block D3 of `[[Discovery Call Script]]`), and ask what happened the *last* time they bought something similar. The last-time question gets the truth; the process question gets the story.

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | We do not know who signs |
| ⚠️ Weak | We know the name; we have never spoken to them |
| ✅ Strong | We have spoken to them directly, and they engaged |

---

### 3 · هيقرروا إزاي — How they'll choose

**What it means:** what they will compare us against and what will make them pick one.

**Why it decides deals:** if they are comparing us to a freelancer on price alone, we lose — unless we change what is being compared. That reframing has to happen before the proposal, not after.

**The four real competitors at this size, in order of frequency:**
1. **Doing nothing** — by far the most common, and the hardest to beat
2. **A freelancer** — `[[Objection Handling]]` #3
3. **A template or ready-made system** — #13, #14
4. **A relative who codes** — #2

Another agency is a distant fifth. Do not plan for a bake-off that is not happening.

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | We don't know what they're weighing us against |
| ⚠️ Weak | We know the alternative but not what would tip the decision |
| ✅ Strong | We know the alternative, the criteria, and we have reframed the comparison |

---

### 4 · خطوات القرار — The steps to yes

**What it means:** the literal sequence between today and a signature, with names and rough dates.

**Why it decides deals:** "هيرد عليا الأسبوع الجاي" is not a step. A deal with no mapped sequence stalls without anyone noticing it has stalled.

**How you get it:**
> **عشان أنظّم نفسي معاك: من دلوقتي لحد ما نبدأ فعلاً، إيه الخطوات اللي المفروض تحصل بالترتيب؟ ومين اللي بيعمل كل خطوة؟**

*(So I can organise myself with you: from now until we actually start, what are the steps that need to happen in order? And who does each step?)*

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | No idea |
| ⚠️ Weak | A vague sense, no names or dates |
| ✅ Strong | Written steps with names and approximate dates, agreed with them |

---

### 5 · الوجع — The pain

**What it means:** what actually breaks if nothing changes.

**Why it decides deals:** it is the only source of urgency. A prospect who is genuinely fine will stay genuinely fine, forever, no matter how good the proposal.

**The test question:** Block B8 — *"لو مبقاش في حاجة اتعملت خالص وفضل الوضع سنة كمان، إيه اللي هيحصل؟"* If the honest answer is "nothing", there is no deal, only a conversation.

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | B8 answer is "nothing much" |
| ⚠️ Weak | Real pain, no time pressure |
| ✅ Strong | Real pain with a real date driving it — a contract, a season, an audit, a growth wall |

---

### 6 · نصيرنا — Our person inside

**What it means:** someone inside the company who wants this to happen and will make the case for it when you are not in the room.

**Why it decides deals:** you are present for maybe two hours of a decision that takes three weeks. Someone else makes the argument for the other 99% of it, or nobody does.

**Three tests of a real champion** — a friendly person is not a champion:
1. Have they given you information they did not have to give?
2. Have they arranged access to someone more senior?
3. Can they explain *in their own words* why this matters to the business?

Two of three = real champion. Fewer = a pleasant contact.

| Level | Test |
|---|---|
| ❌ Missing | Nobody inside is advocating |
| ⚠️ Weak | Someone friendly, but untested against the three tests |
| ✅ Strong | Passes at least two tests |

## Part 2 — The scorecard

Score each element 0 / 1 / 2 (missing / weak / strong). Maximum 12.

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **10–12** | Real deal | Work it hard. Forecast it. |
| **7–9** | Promising, with gaps | Name the gap, assign an action to close it before the proposal. |
| **4–6** | Weak | Do not send a proposal yet. One more conversation to fill the gaps. |
| **0–3** | Not a deal | Disqualify or move to nurture. Be honest with them. |

**Two hard rules that override the total:**
- **Element 5 (pain) = 0 → the deal is not real**, whatever the other scores say.
- **Element 2 (who pays) = 0 at the proposal stage → do not send the proposal.** Get to the buyer first.

### Good fit

| Signal |
|---|
| Maritime / logistics around Port Said — the beachhead, with proof and proximity |
| Education academy or training centre — live demo asset exists |
| Pharmacy, clinic, medical centre — founder domain fluency |
| **Running on Excel and WhatsApp** with a process that clearly outgrew them |
| 5–50 employees, owner-managed, business is real and profitable |
| Owner is reachable and answers questions directly |
| A date with a real driver behind it |
| Came via referral or an existing client's network |
| Already tried something and learned from it — as opposed to never having tried |
| Talks about outcomes ("عايز أعرف الشحنة فين") rather than features |

### Bad fit

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| Wants equity or revenue share | Not a vendor relationship — `[[Objection Handling]]` #5 |
| Asked for a price before describing a problem | Price shopper |
| Refuses any budget signal after all eight indirect questions | No money or no trust |
| No date and no driver | Will stall indefinitely |
| Cannot name who signs, cannot get you to them | No path to a decision |
| Wants an exact clone of a competitor's system, cheaper | Scope dispute pre-loaded |
| Burned two or more vendors, every story entirely the vendor's fault | You are next |
| Wants free design or a mockup before any paid step | Free-work extraction |
| The real problem is a person, not a process | Software gets blamed for a people problem |
| Demands work start before anything is written | The dispute has already begun |
| Budget permanently below the minimum (`TBD`) | Refer out kindly |
| Constant urgency with no corresponding decision speed | Pressure without commitment |

### Walk-away criteria — stop, regardless of deal size

1. They ask us to misrepresent something to a third party.
2. Payment terms would mean funding their build from our own cash.
3. Scope is refused in writing — "we'll agree as we go".
4. Disrespect toward the team during the sales process. It gets worse after signature, never better.
5. The problem they are paying to solve is not one software solves, and they have been told so and insist.
6. Anything legally or regulatorily unclear that the founder has not approved — especially cross-border Gulf contracting, where the answer is currently `TBD` (`[[Objection Handling]]` #25).

**How to walk away well:**
> **بصراحة معاك، أنا مش شايف إن ده هينفع بالشكل ده، ومش عايز أخد فلوسك في حاجة مش هتديك اللي انت عايزه. هبعتلك اللي وصلت له مكتوب، وسيبه معاك. ولو الوضع اتغير، أنا موجود.**

*(Being straight with you — I don't see this working in this form, and I don't want to take your money for something that won't give you what you want. I'll send you what I concluded in writing and leave it with you. If things change, I'm here.)*

Then: Closed Lost with a reason, re-engagement date +6 months.

## Part 3 — The deal review

**Wednesday, 10:00, 30 minutes. The three biggest live deals only.** Ten minutes each. Reviewing everything means reviewing nothing.

### Agenda per deal (10 minutes)

| Min | What |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | State the deal: company, segment, stage, rough size, days since first contact |
| 1–3 | Read the six scores aloud. Say which ones changed since last week and why. |
| 3–5 | **The single biggest risk.** One, not a list. |
| 5–8 | Challenge questions (below). Someone other than the deal owner asks them. |
| 8–10 | Agree the next action, the owner, the date. Write it in the tracker before moving on. |

### The challenge questions

Ask these of the deal owner. The purpose is to find the lie the deal is telling itself.

1. **Why would they buy this at all?** — in their words, not ours.
2. **Why would they buy it now rather than next year?** — if there is no answer, the deal is not real yet.
3. **Why us rather than a freelancer, a template, or nothing?**
4. **Who have we not spoken to who could stop this?** — the unmet sceptic kills more deals than the known objection.
5. **What have they done that cost them something?** — a meeting, a document, an introduction, an internal conversation. Talk is free; effort is a signal.
6. **What is the single thing that would kill this?** — and what are we doing about it this week?
7. **If it went quiet tomorrow, would we know why?** — if not, we do not understand the deal.
8. **Are we forecasting this honestly, or hopefully?**

### Stall detector

| Symptom | Likely cause | Move |
|---|---|---|
| No reply for 10+ days after a proposal | Unmet decision-maker, or an unspoken objection | The honest close message — `[[Lead Intake and CRM]]` touch 7 |
| "لسه بنراجع" three weeks running | No internal advocate | Test the champion against the three tests; if they fail, go around |
| Keeps asking for more detail, never decides | Fear of committing, or no authority | Ask directly: "إيه اللي محتاجه عشان تقول آه؟" |
| Scope keeps growing before signature | No agreed priority | Return to the Blueprint framing; it is the tool for exactly this |
| Repeated price pressure | Value not established | Go back to element 1 — the number is missing or was never confirmed |
| Meetings happen, nothing changes | Comfortable relationship, no urgency | Element 5 is 0. Say so out loud and offer to close the file. |

## Part 4 — Win/loss review

Last Thursday of every month, 45 minutes. **Every deal closed that month, won or lost.** Wins get reviewed too — an unexamined win teaches nothing and is often luck.

### Template

```
مراجعة صفقة — [الشركة] — [التاريخ]
النتيجة:            [ كسبناها / خسرناها ]
القطاع:              [ بحري-لوجستي / تعليم / صحة / أخرى ]
المصدر:              [ channel + source ]
القيمة:              [ TBD ]
المدة من أول تواصل للقرار:   [ عدد الأيام ]
المرحلة اللي ماتت فيها (لو خسرناها):

الدرجات النهائية (من 12):
  الرقم _  · مين بيدفع _  · هيقرروا إزاي _  · خطوات القرار _  · الوجع _  · نصيرنا _   = _ /12

لو كسبناها:

  كان يبان إنها مش هتمشي من الأول؟   [ آه / لأ ]  ← لو آه، ليه كملنا؟
```

### The discipline of logging losses

**A Closed Lost with no reason is a lesson thrown in the bin.** Three rules:

1. **Reason from the fixed list only** (`[[Sales Playbook]]` Part 7). Free text cannot be counted, and what cannot be counted cannot be improved.
2. **Log the reason we believe, not only the reason they gave.** "الميزانية" is the polite Egyptian no for a dozen different things. Record both: `stated_reason` and `real_reason`.
3. **Ask them.** Two or three weeks after the loss, when there is nothing left to sell:

> **أستاذ [الاسم]، عارف إنكم مشيتوا في اتجاه تاني وده قرارك وأنا محترمه تماماً.**
> **سؤال واحد وبصراحة مش عشان أرجع أبيعلك — عشان أتعلم:**
> **إيه الحاجة اللي لو كنا عملناها مختلف كانت هتفرق؟**
> **أي إجابة هتفيدني، حتى لو كانت قاسية.**

*(Mr [name], I know you went in another direction and that's your decision and I completely respect it. One question, and honestly not to sell to you again — to learn: what's the one thing that, had we done it differently, would have made a difference? Any answer helps me, even a harsh one.)*

People answer this far more often than expected, because there is nothing left at stake. It is the cheapest sales training available.

**After 20 closed deals**, review the reason counts. If one reason dominates, that is not twenty individual losses — it is one process problem appearing twenty times, and it is fixable.

## Part 5 — What to review, monthly

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Win rate by segment | Confirms or refutes the beachhead choice |
| Win rate by source | Tells you where to spend the next hour |
| Average days per stage | Finds the stage where deals go to die |
| Average qualification score of won vs lost deals | Calibrates the scorecard against reality |
| Closed Lost reasons, ranked | The single most actionable table in the vault |
| Deals proposed with element 2 = 0 | Should be zero. Any number above zero is a discipline failure. |

**Do not act on fewer than 10 data points.** With five deals you are reading noise and will change a process that was working.
