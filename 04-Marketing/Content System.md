# Content System — Code Square

## For future Claude

This is the **machine**, not the calendar. It answers: what do we make, where does it come from, who makes it, who approves it, and how does one thing become many things. The calendar (`[[Editorial Calendar - First 90 Days]]`) is the output of this system; the templates (`[[Content Templates and Hooks]]`) are the tools it uses.

The governing insight: Code Square already produces good content and gets nothing back. So this system is deliberately **proof-first**. Every pillar is anchored to an asset that already exists or can exist without permission. No pillar is allowed that requires inventing a claim.

Roles below are named as functions (Founder, Producer, Designer, Approver), not people, because the team roster is `TBD` (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §10). At current size, one or two people may hold all four. The hand-offs still matter — they are what stops "we'll post something today" from becoming the process.

---

## 1. The rule that governs everything here

> **No content without a proof asset behind it.** (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §0.5)

Before anything is written, the writer names the artifact that backs it: a project, a screenshot, a wireframe, a document, a client sentence, a number. If the artifact cannot be named, the post is not written. This single rule is what separates the new system from the old one, in which seven service infographics made eight unfalsifiable claims each and earned 1–4 reactions.

## 2. Content pillars

Five pillars. Each is tied to a message pillar from `[[Messaging Framework]]` and to a proof asset that exists or is producible. The percentages are the `proposed` share of output in a normal month (months 3+; months 1–2 are weighted heavily to Pillar 1 because the case-study backlog must be cleared).

| # | Pillar | Message pillar it carries | Proof asset | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **الشغل نفسه — The work** (case studies) | "We build the system your business runs on" | The 4 real projects | 30% |
| 2 | **بنبني على المكشوف — Build in daylight** | "We don't just code, we architect for scale" | Live artifacts: wireframes, discovery docs, architecture decisions, scoping questions | 25% |
| 3 | **وجع الشغل — The operational pain** | "You've outgrown spreadsheets and off-the-shelf tools" | Beachhead workflow knowledge; the Osama Sakr and El Shoush domains | 25% |
| 4 | **إزاي بنفكر — How we decide** (founder POV) | "We're not service providers, we're your technology partner" | The founder's own reasoning; the four-phase methodology | 15% |
| 5 | **الناس والمكان — People and place** | Trust, locality, realness | Team, Port Said, the M Tech Square group, behind-the-scenes | 5% |

### Pillar 1 — الشغل نفسه (The work)

Every published project, told as **problem → approach → result**, with at least one number (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §7.1). This is the pillar that was completely absent from Facebook despite four projects existing.

- **Format:** carousel series, single hero post, website case-study page, PDF one-pager.
- **Non-negotiable:** a number. Where it does not yet exist, the draft carries `[[INSERT REAL NUMBER — see Portfolio and Case Studies]]` and **does not publish** until filled or the claim is rewritten to something provable without a number.
- **Permission:** required for El Shoush and Osama Sakr (see §5). Not required for M Tech Square and Techno Square — this is the group's structural advantage (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §3).

### Pillar 2 — بنبني على المكشوف (Build in daylight)

The most under-used asset in the company and the fastest to produce, because it is a by-product of work already happening. Publish the *process*: the discovery questions asked in a kickoff, a wireframe with the reasoning annotated, an architecture decision and the option rejected, a bug and the fix, a scope cut and why.

**Why this pillar earns 25% of output:**
1. It is proof while results are still thin (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §7.4).
2. It is the only content type that credibly signals "مهندسو أنظمة" rather than "we make websites" — anyone can claim engineering; showing the trade-off is the claim.
3. It has **zero incremental cost** — the artifacts are produced by delivery work regardless. The only cost is remembering to capture them.
4. It cannot be copied by a competitor who has no projects running.

**The capture rule:** the Producer takes one screenshot or photo from every working session — a whiteboard, a Figma frame, a terminal, a meeting agenda. `proposed`: a shared folder `05-Assets/Daylight/YYYY-MM/`. Content is written *from* the folder, never invented for it.

**The confidentiality guard:** never publish client data, credentials, real user records, internal pricing, or anything identifying a client's customers. Blur, redact, or rebuild the artifact with dummy data. When in doubt it goes to the Approver.

### Pillar 3 — وجع الشغل (The operational pain)

Content about the buyer's day, not about software. For the maritime beachhead: crew certificate expiries tracked in Excel, a seafarer's documents living in three WhatsApp groups, a vessel schedule change that nobody logged, a placement history nobody can search, monthly reporting rebuilt by hand.

This pillar is what converts a stranger into someone who thinks *"this person has been inside my office."* It is also the pillar most at risk of drifting back to generic — the test is whether a sentence could have been written by someone who had never met a manning agency. If yes, delete it.

### Pillar 4 — إزاي بنفكر (How we decide)

Founder POV. Opinions with a reason behind them: why a project fails before a line of code is written, why we start with the boring part, why we say no to a scope, why an off-the-shelf tool is sometimes the right answer and we will say so. Published from the founder's own face and name — at this company size, people follow a person before a page.

### Pillar 5 — الناس والمكان (People and place)

Small share, high trust value. The office, Port Said, the group, the team at work. A local B2B buyer wants to know you exist physically and will still exist next year.

## 3. The case-study engine

Four projects exist. Each becomes a **series**, not a post. This is the single highest-value production system in the company.

### 3.1 The engine, per project

One project → one **Source Pack** → many assets.

**Step 1 — Build the Source Pack** (the Producer, with the Founder). One folder per project containing:

- [ ] The problem in the client's own words, before Code Square existed
- [ ] What they were using instead (Excel, paper, WhatsApp, an off-the-shelf tool)
- [ ] The cost of that — time, errors, money, missed opportunities
- [ ] What was actually built, in plain language a non-technical owner understands
- [ ] Three to five key screens (redacted/dummy data)
- [ ] One architecture or product decision worth explaining, and the option rejected
- [ ] **The number** — hours saved per week, placements or bookings processed, errors eliminated, time-to-quote before vs after (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §7.2)
- [ ] One client sentence, verbatim, in Arabic
- [ ] Signed permission (external clients only)
- [ ] Logo usage permission, explicitly separate from story permission

**Step 2 — Write the master case study.** Long form, published on the website. Structure: problem → what it was costing → what we asked in discovery → what we built → the decision that mattered → the result with the number → what we would do next.

**Step 3 — Cut the master into the series** (see §6 repurposing table).

**Step 4 — Publish over 2–3 weeks**, not in one day. A ten-part carousel published as one post is one impression; published as a series, it is ten chances to be seen and it trains the audience to expect the next part. Note that the existing M Tech Square case study was already published this way from ~2026-08-24 (`stated`) — the format was right, the distribution and CTA were missing.

### 3.2 The four projects, sequenced

Ordered by **value to the beachhead × speed to publish**.

| Order | Project | Why this position | Permission | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Osama Sakr Manning Agency** | The single most valuable asset in the company for the chosen beachhead — maritime crewing software, built in a Suez Canal city (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §3). Publish it first and everything after it lands on prepared ground. | **Required — ask week 1** | Manual crewing operations → a system with recruitment, project tracking, live dashboards |
| **2** | **M Tech Square Website** | No permission needed; partly written already; showcases craft and the "إيه اللي ناقص؟" discovery framing that is genuinely good copy (`stated`). | Not required (internal) | A company that built digital presence for others had none itself |
| **3** | **Techno Square Platform** | No permission needed, demo-able live, and it is the proof for beachhead B (education) which runs as the volume engine. | Not required (internal) | Platform + mobile app + dashboard + enrolment, in production |
| **4** | **El Shoush Travel System** | Real external B2B, adjacent to logistics/travel, but less central to the maritime beachhead than #1. | **Required — ask week 1** | Booking and management across languages, with Maps integration |

**Risk and mitigation:** if Osama Sakr declines permission, the entire beachhead content plan loses its anchor. Mitigations, in order: (a) offer an anonymised version — "a manning agency in Port Said" — which keeps the domain proof and loses only the name; (b) offer them something in exchange (a free enhancement, a filmed testimonial in their own marketing); (c) reorder to M Tech Square first and buy time. **Ask in week 1 precisely so there is time to recover.**

### 3.3 The permission ask (`proposed` script, Arabic)

Send by WhatsApp from the founder, not from a page.

> أستاذ [الاسم]، تحية طيبة.
> إحنا في Code Square بنجهّز نعرض شغلنا بشكل رسمي على الموقع والصفحة، وحابين نتكلم عن النظام اللي بنيناه لحضرتك.
> اللي هنشاركه: المشكلة اللي كانت موجودة قبل النظام، إزاي اشتغلنا عليها، والنتيجة. ولو ينفع، رقم واحد بس يوضح الفرق — زي عدد الساعات اللي اتوفرت في الأسبوع أو عدد العمليات اللي بقيت بتتعمل على النظام.
> مش هننشر أي بيانات خاصة بحضرتك أو بعملائك، وكل الصور هتكون ببيانات وهمية.
> وقبل أي نشر، هبعتلك النسخة النهائية تراجعها وتوافق عليها بنفسك.
> ينفع نتكلم في الموضوع؟

Keep the written approval — a WhatsApp message saying "موافق" is a record. File it in the Source Pack.

## 4. Weekly production workflow

Roles are functions; one person may hold several. **Every hand-off is a named person and a day.**

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| **Founder** | Positioning calls, POV content, client permissions, final approval on anything naming a client, the Diagnostic Sessions |
| **Producer** | The calendar, briefs, writing, scheduling, capturing daylight assets, the weekly review |
| **Designer** | Creative to spec — one idea per creative, max 15 words on the image (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §8) |
| **Approver** | Publish/hold decision. Default: Founder. Must be a single named person, not "the team" |
| **Responder** | Comments, DMs, WhatsApp, within the response SLA. Named, with defined hours |

### The week

| Day | Activity | Owner | Hand-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunday** | **Weekly planning, 45 min.** Review last week's numbers against `[[Marketing Strategy]]` §7. Confirm the week's 3 posts against the calendar. Note any daylight asset captured during the week that should displace a planned post. | Producer + Founder | → Briefs to Producer |
| **Monday** | Write all of the week's copy in one sitting. Batch writing beats daily writing — the voice stays consistent and the week cannot be derailed by a busy Tuesday. | Producer | → Copy + creative brief to Designer |
| **Tuesday** | Design the week's creatives. One idea each. Arabic type set in Tajawal, website tokens (`#001F3F` navy, `#A855F7` accent, `#D5B182` gold furniture). | Designer | → Back to Producer by end of day |
| **Wednesday** | Assemble, proof, run the approval check (§5), schedule. Anything naming a client goes to the Founder before scheduling. | Producer → Approver | → Scheduled |
| **Thursday** | Publish + **the first 60 minutes matter most**: the Responder is present for the first hour of every post to answer comments. Early engagement is what makes the platform show a post to more people. | Responder | — |
| **Daily** | Capture one daylight asset. Answer every DM/comment within the SLA. | Whole team / Responder | → `05-Assets/Daylight/` |
| **Friday** | Direct outreach block + referral asks. Not posting — this is the highest-converting channel and needs protected time. | Founder | → Logged in CRM |

**The batching rule:** if the week's writing does not happen on Monday, the week does not get three posts, it gets one on Thursday written in a hurry. This has already been the failure mode. Protect Monday.

### Monthly

- **Monthly review (60 min, first Sunday).** North star: qualified Diagnostic Sessions. Which pillar produced enquiries — not which produced likes. Kill the worst-performing format; double the best. Re-check that the content still speaks only to the beachhead.
- **Source Pack replenishment.** Is there at least one project's worth of unpublished material in the bank? If not, the next month will be thin, and that is discovered *now*, not in week 3.

## 5. Approval workflow

### 5.1 The three gates

**Gate A — Truth check** (Producer, on every single post, no exceptions):

- [ ] Every claim traces to a named proof asset
- [ ] No number is invented. Placeholders are `[[INSERT REAL NUMBER — see Portfolio and Case Studies]]` and **block publication**
- [ ] No banned phrase (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §5): أقوى فريق · أفضل شركة · حلول متكاملة · أحدث التقنيات · ثقة عملائنا · Awwwards-tier · 99.9% uptime · SLA Guarantees · worldwide/globally
- [ ] No client name, logo, or screenshot without permission on file
- [ ] Only one CTA, and it is the Diagnostic Session or a question
- [ ] Creative carries one idea, ≤15 words
- [ ] Arabic is written natively, not machine-translated (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §0.3)

**Gate B — Client permission** (mandatory before any case study naming an external client):

1. Founder asks for permission in principle (§3.3) → record the answer
2. Draft written → **sent to the client in full, in Arabic, before publication**
3. Client approves the exact text, screenshots, and the number → written record kept in the Source Pack
4. Logo usage confirmed **separately** — permission to tell the story is not permission to use the mark
5. Any client edit is accepted without argument. A weaker true story beats a stronger lost relationship
6. Only then does it enter the schedule

> **Hard rule: no external-client content is scheduled before step 4 is complete.** Not "scheduled pending approval". Not scheduled at all. This is the rule most likely to be bent under calendar pressure, and the one with the worst downside — the beachhead depends on the Osama Sakr relationship staying good.

**Gate C — Founder sign-off** required for: anything naming a client, pricing, any claim about capability the company has not shipped, anything about the M Tech Square group, and all paid creative.

### 5.2 Escalation

Producer unsure → Approver. Approver unsure about a client → the client. Nobody guesses. The cost of a delayed post is one day; the cost of a wrongly published client detail is the reference the whole beachhead rests on.

## 6. Repurposing rules

**The rule: nothing is made once.** Every substantial asset has a mandatory distribution list. This is the difference between the current output rate and the required one, without adding hours.

### One case study → 14 assets

| # | Asset | Channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Master case study page | Website | The canonical version everything links to |
| 2 | Hero post — the problem | Facebook | Hook from `[[Content Templates and Hooks]]` |
| 3 | Carousel — problem → approach → result (6–8 frames) | Facebook | One idea per frame |
| 4 | Post — the discovery question we asked | Facebook | Pillar 2 crossover |
| 5 | Post — the decision we made and what we rejected | Facebook | Pillar 2 crossover |
| 6 | Post — the number, alone, as a single creative | Facebook | Highest-performing format when a real number exists |
| 7 | Client quote card | Facebook + Instagram | Verbatim Arabic, attributed |
| 8 | Screen walkthrough video, 45–60s | Facebook + Instagram Reels | Screen recording + voiceover; no studio needed |
| 9 | English version of the master | Website + LinkedIn | Natively written, for Gulf/enterprise |
| 10 | LinkedIn post — founder voice, first person | LinkedIn | "Here is what we learned building X" |
| 11 | PDF one-pager | Email, WhatsApp, outreach | The single most useful sales asset the company can own |
| 12 | Outreach message referencing it | WhatsApp / email | "We built this for an agency in your industry" |
| 13 | Story sequence | Instagram + Facebook Stories | Cut from the carousel frames |
| 14 | Pinned-post candidate | Facebook | The best-performing one becomes the pinned post |

### Other repurposing paths

| Source | Becomes |
|---|---|
| One Diagnostic Session (anonymised, with consent) | 1 pain post + 1 POV post + 1 FAQ post |
| One client question in DMs | 1 Q&A post + 1 website FAQ entry + 1 outreach hook |
| One architecture decision | 1 daylight post + 1 LinkedIn post + a paragraph in the next case study |
| One wireframe or Figma frame | 1 daylight post + 1 story + 1 carousel frame later |
| One long POV post that performed | 1 short video + 1 LinkedIn version + 1 English version |
| One industry event attended | 1 photo post + 3 outreach conversations + 1 pain post from what was overheard |

**Repurposing is not reposting.** Each version is rewritten for its channel — length, opening line, and language. Copy-pasting a Facebook caption onto LinkedIn is how a company signals it is not paying attention.

## 7. Asset checklist per content type

Nothing enters the schedule with an incomplete checklist.

**Single image post**
- [ ] Creative, 1080×1080 or 1080×1350, one idea, ≤15 words on the image
- [ ] Caption, Egyptian dialect, hook in the first line (the line visible before "see more")
- [ ] Proof asset named in the brief
- [ ] One CTA
- [ ] Link (once the site is live) + UTM tags
- [ ] Alt text in Arabic

**Carousel**
- [ ] 6–10 frames, one idea per frame, ≤15 words each
- [ ] Frame 1 = the hook. Frame 2 = the stakes. Final frame = the CTA
- [ ] Frames readable at thumbnail size on a phone
- [ ] Consistent template — same grid, same type scale
- [ ] Caption that stands alone if the images are never swiped

**Case study (any format)**
- [ ] Complete Source Pack (§3.1)
- [ ] Permission on file (external clients)
- [ ] A real number, or the placeholder and a hold on publication
- [ ] Screenshots redacted / dummy data
- [ ] Client approval of the exact final text
- [ ] Website page live and linked

**Video (45–90s)**
- [ ] Script — hook in the first 3 seconds, one idea, named next step
- [ ] **Burned-in Arabic subtitles** — assume sound-off viewing
- [ ] Vertical 9:16 for Reels/Stories, square or 4:5 for feed
- [ ] Clean audio (phone mic close, or a lapel mic — the cheapest quality upgrade available)
- [ ] Thumbnail with ≤5 words
- [ ] First frame legible as a still

**Daylight / behind-the-scenes**
- [ ] Real artifact, captured not staged
- [ ] Confidentiality check passed — no client data, credentials, or internal pricing
- [ ] The *reasoning* explained, not just the picture shown
- [ ] Ends with a question

**Founder POV**
- [ ] One opinion, stated in the first line
- [ ] A reason, an example, and an honest concession of the other side
- [ ] Written in the founder's voice — read aloud before publishing
- [ ] No hedging into a capability list

**Outreach / PDF one-pager**
- [ ] Their industry named on page one
- [ ] The relevant case study, with its number
- [ ] Named next step: the Diagnostic Session
- [ ] Contact details that work — phone, WhatsApp, email, site link
- [ ] Under 2 pages, readable on a phone
