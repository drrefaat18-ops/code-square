# Competitive Landscape

## For future Claude

This note answers one question: **when an Egyptian SMB buyer decides to fix a manual workflow, what are they actually choosing between?** Not "who are the other agencies" — the real competitive set includes freelancers, ready-made software, and, most importantly, **doing nothing**.

Tagging as everywhere in this vault: **stated** · **researched** (URL + date) · **proposed** · **TBD**.

⚠️ **No named competitor is analysed here from private knowledge.** Where a specific local firm matters, it is listed as a category with a **TBD** instruction to research it properly. Do not let a future version of this note invent competitor revenue, headcount, or client lists.

---

## The eight real competitors

Ordered by how often they actually win.

| # | Competitor | Typical price to the buyer | Wins when | Loses when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Doing nothing** (Excel + WhatsApp) | EGP 0 | Always the default | A dated external trigger appears |
| 2 | **Freelancers** (Mostaql, Khamsat, Upwork, personal networks) | EGP 5k–50k | Scope is small and clear | Anything needs to still work in month seven |
| 3 | **Local Port Said / Delta agencies** | EGP 15k–80k | Buyer wants a face nearby | Buyer needs engineering, not a website |
| 4 | **Cairo / Alexandria dev shops** | EGP 100k–1m+ | Buyer is large and formal | Ticket is small; travel and account minimums do not fit |
| 5 | **Ready-made SaaS & site builders** (Wix, Shopify, Zoho, vertical SaaS) | EGP 500–5k / month | The problem is generic | The workflow is genuinely specific |
| 6 | **Odoo / ERP implementers** | EGP 125k–350k+ | Buyer wants accounting + inventory + everything | Buyer's core workflow is not an ERP module |
| 7 | **Local Egyptian desktop packages** (pharmacy, accounting, customs) | EGP 5k–40k one-off | Cheap, familiar, everyone in the trade uses it | Multi-branch, multi-user, or web/mobile is needed |
| 8 | **An internal hire** ("we'll get a developer") | EGP 15k–40k / month salary | Buyer is big enough to keep one busy | One developer cannot design, build, and run a system alone |

## 1. Doing nothing — Excel, WhatsApp, and a shared drive

**This is the incumbent in every single deal. It has ~95% market share and it is free.** Any pitch that does not beat "keep doing what we're doing" loses regardless of how it compares to other vendors.

| | |
|---|---|
| **How it wins** | Zero cost, zero risk, zero change management. It already works — badly, but visibly. Nobody was ever fired for keeping the Excel sheet. |
| **How it loses** | It fails at scale, at handover, at multi-site, at audit, and at any externally imposed deadline. It concentrates the whole business in one employee's head. |
| **Real cost, invisible to the buyer** | Interrupt labour, rework, expiry write-offs, margin leakage between estimate and invoice, and total key-person risk. |
| **How Code Square beats it** | **Never argue that Excel is bad — quantify what it costs.** Ask: how many hours per week does someone spend answering "where is my X?" Multiply by the salary. That number, said back to the buyer in their own figures, is the whole sale. Then attach a **dated external trigger** (ETA e-invoicing on 31 March 2026, a new branch, a principal's demand) so inertia has a deadline. |

**Framing line (AR):** *الإكسل مش المشكلة. المشكلة إن الشركة كلها ماشية في دماغ موظف واحد.*

## 2. Freelancers — Mostaql, Khamsat, Upwork, and "ابن عمي بيبرمج"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price positioning** | Mostaql projects for a full website run roughly **$500–$3,000** (**researched** — [jobbers.io, 2026](https://www.jobbers.io/mostaql-vs-khamsat-vs-jobbers-best-arabic-freelance-platform-2026-complete-review/), accessed 2026-09-02). Khamsat starts at **$5** per gig. Egyptian freelance web hourly is roughly **EGP 200–500** (**researched** — [queentechsolutions.net](https://www.queentechsolutions.net/web-development-2/website-development-cost/), accessed 2026-09-02). |
| **How they win** | Price, and speed on a clear small brief. For a landing page or a simple form, a freelancer is genuinely the correct answer and Code Square should say so. |
| **How they lose** | No architecture, no documentation, no handover, no availability in month seven, no accountability when it breaks, and no ability to hold a business conversation about the workflow. The known failure mode: the system works, then the freelancer becomes unreachable and nobody can change it. Every SMB owner has either lived this or heard about it. |
| **Framing** | Do not compete on price and do not disparage freelancers. Compete on **continuity and ownership**. The question that ends this comparison: *"بعد ٦ شهور، لما تحتاج تعديل، هتكلم مين؟"* |
| **Where Code Square must be honest** | If the buyer's real need is a brochure website, a freelancer *is* cheaper and better for them. Say it and refer it out. That referral is worth more in reputation than the margin on a job that does not fit. See the disqualifiers in `[[ICP - Ideal Customer Profiles]]`. |

## 4. Cairo and Alexandria development companies

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price positioning** | Egyptian software companies bill roughly **$25–$39/hr**, with a median around **$37/hr** across the country's better-known firms, and individual firms ranging much higher (**researched** — [thescalers.com](https://thescalers.com/offshore-software-development-rates-by-country/); [insigniaresource.com](https://www.insigniaresource.com/research/outsourcing-rates-by-country/), accessed 2026-09-02). Egypt's software sector is deep — public directories list very large numbers of firms (**researched** — [goodfirms.co Egypt directory](https://www.goodfirms.co/directory/country/top-software-development-companies/egypt); [designrush.com](https://www.designrush.com/agency/software-development/eg), accessed 2026-09-02). |
| **How they win** | Scale, brand, formal process, real teams, and comfort for a buyer who needs a procurement-safe choice. On any large or enterprise deal, they win. |
| **How they lose** | They are optimised for export and enterprise clients, not for a 20-person Port Said agency. A Port Said SMB is below their account minimum, gets a junior team if it gets one at all, and pays for travel and overhead. They also have **no maritime vertical familiarity in Port Said specifically**. |
| **Framing** | *"الشركة الكبيرة في القاهرة هتبعتلك فريق مبتدئ وحساب صغير. إحنا على بعد ١٥ دقيقة من مكتبك، والحساب ده هو الحساب المهم عندنا."* Proximity and attention, not capability, is the argument — do not claim to out-engineer a 200-person firm. |

## 5. Ready-made SaaS and site builders — Wix, Shopify, Zoho, vertical SaaS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price positioning** | Subscription. Zoho One runs roughly **$37–$45 per employee/month** (all-employee) or **$90–$105 per user/month** (flexible) (**researched** — [pptssolutions.com, 2026](https://www.pptssolutions.com/blogs/odoo-vs-zoho-one-erp-for-smes), accessed 2026-09-02). Site builders are far cheaper. |
| **How they win** | Instant, cheap, no project risk, no vendor dependency. **For generic problems — a website, a CRM, email, a store — they are simply the right answer** and Code Square should recommend them without embarrassment. |
| **How they lose** | Egyptian-specific and vertical-specific workflows do not fit. Arabic and RTL support is often mediocre. Local payment rails (InstaPay, Fawry, Vodafone Cash) and ETA e-invoicing compliance are usually absent or awkward. Per-user pricing in **USD** is punishing for an Egyptian buyer earning EGP — at roughly EGP 51/USD (**researched** — [XE, 2026-09-02](https://www.xe.com/en-us/currencyconverter/convert/?Amount=1&From=EGP&To=USD)), a 20-person Zoho One deployment is a serious recurring foreign-currency expense. |
| **Framing** | **Do not fight SaaS — integrate with it.** The credible position is: use the ready-made tool where it fits, and let Code Square build the 20% that is genuinely specific and connect the two. That is exactly what "تكامل الأنظمة" already claims on the site (**stated**). It also turns the strongest competitor into a component of the offer. |
| **The currency argument** | This is Code Square's most underused competitive weapon and it is entirely factual: EGP-denominated pricing against a USD subscription that reprices every time the pound moves. |

## 6. Odoo and ERP implementers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price positioning** | A basic Odoo deployment for a small Egyptian business runs **EGP 125,000–350,000** (**researched** — [buildn.tech, 2026](https://buildn.tech/en/blog/erp-implementation-cost-egypt-2026), accessed 2026-09-02); globally, most SME implementations land between **$15,000 and $60,000** all-in, with licences only 30–40% of total cost (**researched** — [silentinfotech.com, 2026](https://silentinfotech.com/blog/odoo-1/odoo-erp-implementation-cost-guide-2026-459), accessed 2026-09-02). Egypt has an established ERP partner ecosystem (**researched** — [azdan.com partner directory, 2026](https://www.azdan.com/blog/erp-companies-egypt)). |
| **How they win** | When the buyer's need really is accounting, inventory, purchasing, and HR, an ERP is a better answer than custom software and Code Square should say so. ERP also carries board-level credibility. |
| **How they lose** | Long implementations, painful customisation, and a poor fit for a workflow that is not an ERP module — crew certificate rotation, a port call file, a term enrolment cycle. Buyers who force a specific workflow into a generic ERP end up paying twice. |
| **Framing** | **This is Code Square's most important price anchor.** A custom V1 priced at EGP 150k–500k (proposed in `[[ICP - Ideal Customer Profiles]]` A.5) sits *inside a band the market already accepts for a generic ERP* while delivering the workflow the ERP cannot. Line: *"الـ ERP هيديك المحاسبة. مش هيديك متابعة الشهادات."* |

## 7. Local Egyptian desktop packages

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Long-established desktop applications for pharmacies, accounting, and customs clearance, sold as a one-off licence with a USB key and a phone number for support. **TBD** — name and price the specific incumbents in pharmacy and in customs before pitching Segments A and C. |
| **How they win** | Cheap, familiar, everyone in the trade already uses one, and the sales rep is a known person. Switching costs are emotional as much as technical. |
| **How they lose** | Single-machine or single-branch. No web, no mobile, no consolidated multi-branch view, no API, and often struggling with ETA e-invoicing requirements. |
| **Framing** | **Never propose replacing them in the first conversation.** Position as the **layer above** — the consolidation, reporting, and mobile access that the desktop package structurally cannot provide. This is precisely the Dr. Nourhan objection in `[[Buyer Personas]]`. |

## 8. Hiring a developer in-house

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price positioning** | A salaried developer. **TBD** — confirm current Port Said and Cairo salary bands rather than assuming. |
| **How it wins** | Feels cheaper over 12 months, and gives the owner a person in the building. |
| **How it loses** | One developer is not a team. They cannot do discovery, UX, architecture, backend, mobile, and operations at once. They have no peer review, they take annual leave, and when they resign the company owns code nobody can read. Hiring, managing, and retaining a developer is itself a skill the buyer does not have. |
| **Framing** | **This objection is the exact reason the "Outsourced CTO" positioning exists** (**stated** — it is already point 7 on the website). Line: *"فريق كامل بتكلفة أقل من موظف واحد — ومن غير ما تدير حد."* This is the single claim a freelancer and a template shop cannot make, per `[[Foundation Brief]]` §3. |

## Positioning map

Two axes chosen because they are what the buyer actually weighs: **how much of the price is paid for engineering depth**, and **how specific the solution is to their workflow**.

```
                        HIGH PRICE / DEEP ENGINEERING
                                    ▲
                                    │
        Cairo & Alexandria      ●   │
        dev shops                   │
                                    │        ● Odoo / ERP
                                    │          implementers
                                    │
  GENERIC ◄───────────────────┬─────┼───────────────────────► SPECIFIC TO
  (off-the-shelf)             │     │        ★ CODE SQUARE      THE WORKFLOW
                              │     │          (target position)
              ● Wix / Shopify │     │
              ● Zoho / SaaS   │     │   ● Local desktop packages
                              │     │     (pharmacy, customs)
        ● "Do nothing"        │     │
          Excel + WhatsApp    │     │   ● Local Port Said agencies
                              │     │   ● Freelancers
                                    │        ▲
                                    ▼        └── Code Square is
                        LOW PRICE / SHALLOW      currently perceived
                                                 HERE on Facebook
```

**Read the map this way.** The bottom-right quadrant — workflow-specific but cheap and shallow — is where freelancers and small local agencies live, and where **Facebook currently places Code Square**. The website places it in the **upper right**: specific *and* engineered. That gap between the two positions is the coherence problem named in `[[Foundation Brief]]` §2, restated here as a competitive fact rather than a branding one: **Code Square is currently competing in a price fight it did not choose and cannot win.**

The upper-right quadrant is where the maritime and healthcare verticals live, where the ERP price anchor protects the ticket size, and where the four-project portfolio is the entry ticket. **Nobody else in Port Said occupies it** (**proposed** — verify against the local agency list once built).

## How to frame Code Square against each competitor — one line each

| Against | The line (AR) | The line (EN) |
|---|---|---|
| Doing nothing | الإكسل مش المشكلة — المشكلة إن الشركة ماشية في دماغ موظف واحد | Excel isn't the problem. The problem is that the company runs inside one employee's head. |
| A freelancer | بعد ٦ شهور، لما تحتاج تعديل، هتكلم مين؟ | In six months, when you need a change, who do you call? |
| A local agency | إحنا مش بنعمل مواقع. إحنا بنبني النظام اللي شغلك ماشي عليه | We don't build websites. We build the system your business runs on. |
| A Cairo firm | حسابك عندهم صغير. عندنا هو الحساب | You're a small account there. Here, you're the account. |
| Ready-made SaaS | استخدمه في اللي هو كويس فيه — وإحنا نبني الجزء اللي مالوش بديل، ونوصّلهم ببعض | Use it for what it's good at. We'll build the part that has no substitute, and connect them. |
| Odoo / ERP | الـ ERP هيديك المحاسبة. مش هيديك متابعة الشهادات | An ERP gives you accounting. It doesn't give you certificate tracking. |
| Desktop packages | مش هنشيل برنامجك. هنبني الطبقة اللي بتشوف كل الفروع فوقه | We're not removing your software. We're building the layer above it that sees every branch. |
| An in-house hire | فريق كامل بتكلفة أقل من موظف واحد — ومن غير ما تدير حد | A whole team for less than one salary, and you manage nobody. |

## What would change this analysis

| Signal to watch | Why it matters |

| A local Port Said agency hiring real engineers and publishing case studies | The only local threat that matters |
| The EGP/USD rate moving sharply | Changes the SaaS-versus-custom argument in both directions — currently Code Square's friend |
