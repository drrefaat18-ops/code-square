# Beachhead Decision

## For future Claude

**This is a decision document, not an essay.** It scores the four candidate segments from `[[Foundation Brief]]` §4 against weighted criteria and makes one recommendation. Until the founder signs off, this note stays `status: awaiting-founder-decision` and **nothing downstream — ads, landing pages, sales scripts, content calendar — should be built as if the decision is made.**

Scores are **proposed** (analysis, 2026-09-02). The inputs are **stated** (Code Square's own evidence notes) and **researched** (external, cited in `[[ICP - Ideal Customer Profiles]]` and `[[Competitive Landscape]]`). Nothing about Code Square's revenue, headcount, or client count is claimed here, because none of it is known.

When the founder decides: change `status:` to `decided`, record the date and the choice, and update `[[Open Questions and Decisions Needed]]`.

---

## Jargon, defined once

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Beachhead** | The one narrow market you attack first, win completely, and then expand from. Named after an amphibious landing: you take one beach, not the whole coast. |
| **Weighted score** | Each criterion is scored 1–5, then multiplied by how much that criterion matters. Stops a strong score on a trivial criterion outranking a weak score on a decisive one. |
| **Ticket size** | The money in one deal. |
| **Competitive intensity** | How many credible alternatives the buyer already has. Lower is better for you. |
| **Expansion potential** | Whether winning here opens a bigger market later, or is a dead end. |

---

## The decision being made

> **Which ONE of the four segments does Code Square lead with for the next 6–9 months** — in its Facebook bio, its cover, its pinned post, its landing page, its outreach, and its first paid ad?

**What this decision is not.** It is not a decision to refuse work from the other three. Code Square can and should still take a good project from any segment that walks in. A beachhead governs where **attention and marketing spend** go, not where invoices come from. Per `[[Foundation Brief]]` §0: strategy is a choice about what *not* to promote.

---

## Criteria and weights

Seven criteria. The weights reflect Code Square's actual situation as of 2026-09-02: **no distribution, no published proof, unknown delivery capacity, and a founder who must personally do the selling.**

| # | Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proof held today** | **25%** | The heaviest weight, deliberately. `[[Foundation Brief]]` §7 makes proof the bottleneck for the whole company. A segment where a real shipped project already exists shortens time-to-first-sale more than any other factor. Proof you have to go create is a 6-month delay. |
| 2 | **Ticket size** | **20%** | With no headcount known and no ad budget confirmed, few deals must produce real revenue. Twenty small deals is not a viable plan for a team of unknown size. |
| 3 | **Competitive intensity** (lower is better) | **15%** | Per `[[Competitive Landscape]]`, most segments put Code Square in a price fight against freelancers and templates it cannot win. A segment with few credible alternatives protects the ticket. |
| 4 | **Founder access / network** | **15%** | There is no sales team. Every first meeting happens because the founder can get into the room. Access is not a soft factor here; it is the distribution channel. |
| 5 | **Sales-cycle length** (shorter is better) | **10%** | Matters, but is deliberately weighted *below* ticket size and proof. A short cycle on a small ticket is a treadmill, not a strategy. |
| 6 | **Delivery capability** | **10%** | Can Code Square actually build and support this well, with what it has? A won deal it cannot deliver produces a bad reference, which is worse than no reference. |
| 7 | **Expansion potential** | **5%** | Real, but lowest weight. A company with 74 followers should not optimise for a market it will reach in year three. |

**Weights sum to 100%.** They are a judgement call and the founder may change them. If he does, re-run the table — that is the whole point of showing the arithmetic.

## 🎯 RECOMMENDATION

> ### Lead with **Segment A — Maritime, logistics & port services**, starting with **crewing/manning agencies and shipping agencies in Port Said**.
>
> ### Runner-up: **Segment B — Education**, which continues as the **demo engine and content asset**, not as the marketing headline.

This matches the recommendation already in `[[Foundation Brief]]` §4. The scoring did not merely confirm it — it strengthened it, because two independent factors point the same way: **A is the only segment with both a shipped reference and low competitive intensity**, and it is the only one where a competitor structurally cannot follow.

### What "lead with A" concretely means

| Asset | What changes |
|---|---|
| Facebook bio, cover, pinned post | Rewritten to name maritime and port operations explicitly. Not "شريكك التكنولوجي" — a named workflow. |
| Landing page | One page for maritime operations software, not a services list. See `[[Brand Positioning]]`. |
| First case study published | **Osama Sakr Manning Agency, with at least one number** (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §7 step 1–2). This is the highest-priority piece of content in the company. |
| Outreach list | The **Port Said Chamber of Shipping member list** (**researched** — [pscsegypt.com/list.php](http://www.pscsegypt.com/list.php), accessed 2026-09-02), segmented by sub-type. |
| Offer | The diagnostic session, framed as a **port-call / crewing workflow review**, not a generic consultation. |
| Ad spend | **Zero until Priority 0 in `[[Foundation Brief]]` §9 is done.** The expired TLS certificate and the unrendered site make any spend a waste. |

### What is being deprioritised — stated explicitly

Per `[[Foundation Brief]]` §0, a strategy without deprioritisation is a wish list. Choosing A means:

1. **Segment D gets no marketing at all.** It becomes a referral partnership only.
2. **Segment C gets one pilot, not a campaign.** One founder-network project, traded for a documented before/after and a filmed testimonial. It is a proof-building exercise, not a market entry.
3. **Segment B stays visible but is not the headline.** Techno Square remains the demo and the content engine, and inbound education deals are welcome — but no ad spend and no repositioning around it.
4. **The seven-service capability list stops being the message.** It stays in the service catalog only (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §0 rule 4).
5. **Gulf expansion is deferred.** Jeddah and Riyadh stay on the service-area list but get no dedicated effort until A is winning in Egypt. See `[[Market Map - Egypt and Gulf]]`.

## The honest case against the recommendation

A good decision document argues the other side. Three real objections:

| Objection | Weight of the objection | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **A has the longest sales cycle.** With unknown runway, 4–8 months to a first cold deal could be fatal. | **Serious.** This is the strongest argument against A. | Mitigate by running B for near-term cashflow while A is the marketing position. The two are not exclusive — B deals are fast and demoable; A deals are what the brand is built on. **If runway is under 6 months, this objection wins and the answer flips to B.** The founder must answer the runway question. |
| **A rests on ONE client relationship.** If Osama Sakr will not give permission to be named or to share a number, the proof advantage evaporates. | **Serious and testable this week.** | **This is the single highest-priority validation.** Ask before committing marketing spend. If permission is refused, A's proof score falls from 5 to ~2 and the total drops to roughly **3.60 — a statistical tie with B**, and B wins on cycle and delivery. |
| **C has the best founder access, and access is what actually produces meetings.** | Fair. | Access without proof produces meetings that do not close, and a pharmacist selling software to pharmacists still has to show a working system. C is one pilot away from being a genuine contender — which is exactly what is recommended for it. |

## What evidence would change this recommendation

| Evidence | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Osama Sakr refuses permission to be named or to share any number** | A's proof score → 2; total → ~3.60. **Switch to B**, and revisit A once a second maritime reference exists. |
| **Runway is under 6 months, or the founder is not full-time on Code Square** | A's cycle length becomes disqualifying. **Switch to B** for cashflow; keep A as the 12-month position. |
| **The B2G project turns out to be real and public** (**TBD** — `[[Foundation Brief]]` §1) | Materially strengthens A, or opens a fifth government/public-sector segment worth scoring separately. |
| **A Cairo firm or an international vendor launches an Egyptian maritime product** | A's competitive-intensity score falls from 5 to ~2; total → ~3.90. Still ahead of B, but the window narrows and speed becomes critical. |
| **Two or more maritime prospects say the price band is unaffordable** | Ticket score falls; re-price in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` before abandoning the segment. Do not confuse a pricing error with a segment error. |
| **A healthcare pilot closes and produces a hard number** (e.g. EGP X of expiry write-off avoided) | C jumps from 2.70 to roughly 3.70 and becomes the credible runner-up ahead of B — because the founder's access is real and the ticket is bigger. |
| **Delivery capacity turns out to be one person part-time** | Every segment's delivery score drops, and the correct answer becomes B — the only one with a product that already exists. |
