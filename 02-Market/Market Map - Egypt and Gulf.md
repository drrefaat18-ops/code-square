# Market Map — Egypt and Gulf

## For future Claude

The demand picture behind `[[ICP - Ideal Customer Profiles]]` and `[[Beachhead Decision]]`: **why Egyptian SMBs are buying software now, what Port Said specifically offers, and what selling into Saudi Arabia actually requires.**

Code Square lists **Jeddah and Riyadh** as service areas (**stated** — `[[2026-09-02 - Facebook Page Evidence]]`) while publishing **no English content at all**. This note exists partly to make the cost of that gap explicit.

Tagging as elsewhere: **stated** · **researched** (URL + date) · **proposed** · **TBD / NEEDS VERIFICATION**. Where a figure could not be verified, it is marked, not guessed. Rates and regulations move fast — **re-verify anything financial or legal before acting on it.**

### 1.3 Geography inside Egypt

| Market | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Port Said** | Home market. Small, dense, relationship-driven, and **maritime**. Code Square's structural advantage: it can be in the buyer's office within the hour. Downside: a small absolute number of firms — the market is finite and must not be burned with bad outreach. |
| **Damietta, Ismailia, Suez, Ain Sokhna** | The natural ring-two expansion. Same industries, same canal, same language, drivable. **This is the growth path for Segment A, and it is far cheaper than Saudi.** |
| **Alexandria** | Egypt's second port and second tech hub. Larger maritime market, but also local competitors and more supply. |
| **Cairo / Giza** | Biggest market, most competition, no local advantage. Best treated as inbound-only until a vertical position is established. |

## Part 2 — Port Said and the Suez Canal Economic Zone

### 2.1 Why the location matters commercially

Port Said sits at the Mediterranean entrance to the Suez Canal. That single fact produces a dense local cluster of shipping agencies, crewing agencies, freight forwarders, customs brokers, ship chandlers, and bunkering suppliers — the businesses profiled as Segment A in `[[ICP - Ideal Customer Profiles]]`.

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The Suez Canal Economic Zone (SCZone) drew roughly **$7bn** across **117 projects**, expected to support **73,000+ jobs** | **researched** — [AGBI, Aug 2026](https://www.agbi.com/logistics/2026/08/egypts-suez-canal-economic-zone-draws-7bn-from-investors/) |
| SCZone investment reached **$7.1bn** in the fiscal year per its chairperson | **researched** — [Daily News Egypt, May 2026](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/2026/05/04/suez-canal-economic-zone-investments-hit-7-1bn-so-far-this-fiscal-year-chairperson-tells-oecd/) |
| **AD Ports Group** signed a 50-year renewable agreement to develop a **20 km² industrial and logistics park near Port Said** | **researched** — [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/shipping/ports/ad-ports-group-suez-canal-economic-zone-to-develop-logistics-park-at-port-said); [saudigulfprojects.com, 2025](https://www.saudigulfprojects.com/2025/05/ad-ports-group-and-suez-canal-economic-zone-signs-agreement-to-develop-kezad-east-port-said-zone/) |
| **East Port Said** reported as one of the region's leading container terminals, projected around **5.6 million containers in 2026** | **researched** — surfaced in SCZone reporting, accessed 2026-09-02. **NEEDS VERIFICATION against a primary port-authority source before publishing this number.** |
| A **published, named member list** of Port Said shipping firms exists | **researched** — [Port Said Chamber of Shipping](http://www.pscsegypt.com/list.php), accessed 2026-09-02 |
| A named Port Said ship-supplier directory exists | **researched** — [ISSA Port Said](https://shipsupply.org/country/egypt/port-said/), accessed 2026-09-02 |

### 2.2 What this means, stripped of the excitement

Growth in a port zone is **not automatically demand for custom software** — most of that investment is in terminals, industrial plant, and logistics real estate, not in SME back-office systems. **Do not put "$7bn SCZone investment" in a sales deck as if it were Code Square's market.** That is the kind of unfalsifiable framing `[[Foundation Brief]]` §5 bans.

The honest, usable version is narrower and better:

> **More vessel calls and more cargo through Port Said means the existing agencies handle more volume with the same staff.** That capacity squeeze — not the headline investment figure — is what makes them buy.

### 2.3 The single most actionable fact in this note

A **public, named, addressable list of the exact target companies for the recommended beachhead already exists** at the Port Said Chamber of Shipping. Most companies never get this. Code Square has it, in its own city, for free.

**Action:** transcribe and segment that list by sub-type (shipping agency / crewing / forwarder / broker / chandler / bunkering), then work it. This belongs in `[[Sales Playbook]]`.

## Part 3 — The Gulf: what Jeddah and Riyadh actually require

### 3.1 Why they are on the list

They are listed as service areas on the Facebook page (**stated**). Nothing in the evidence notes explains why, and there is **no stated Gulf client, no Gulf reference, and no English content** (**stated**). Two plausible reasons, both **TBD until the founder answers**:

1. Aspiration — Saudi is where the money is.
2. An existing personal or diaspora connection the founder has not documented.

**Which of these is true changes the plan completely.** A real contact is a beachhead. An aspiration is a distraction. → `[[Open Questions and Decisions Needed]]`.

### 3.2 What Saudi genuinely offers

| Factor | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Price level** | Materially higher than Egypt. Exact software price bands are **TBD** — do not assume a multiple without evidence. |
| **Currency** | The **SAR is pegged to the USD** (long-standing peg — **researched**, widely documented; verify the current rate before quoting). For an Egyptian vendor earning EGP, SAR revenue is effectively **hard-currency revenue** — the strongest single argument for Gulf work. |
| **Maritime relevance** | Jeddah Islamic Port and the Eastern Province ports run the same industry as Port Said: agencies, forwarders, brokers, crewing. **A maritime vertical position built in Port Said is directly portable — the vocabulary transfers, the workflows transfer, the proof transfers.** This is why Segment A scores 5 on expansion in `[[Beachhead Decision]]`. |
| **Demand climate** | Large, well-funded digitisation programmes across Saudi government and enterprise. Also means **strong local and international competition** at every level. |

### 3.3 What selling into Saudi actually requires

| Requirement | Detail | Difficulty for Code Square |
|---|---|---|
| **A legal presence, for anything but small remote work** | Foreign companies invest via a **MISA licence** plus a Commercial Registration. For service activities, reporting suggests capital requirements have been relaxed substantially — figures around **SAR 25,000** for some service licence types, with setup often quoted at 2–3 weeks (**researched** — [equimerger.com](https://www.equimerger.com/blog/misa-licence-saudi-arabia); [mypronexa.com](https://mypronexa.com/misa-license-in-saudi-arabia/), accessed 2026-09-02. **NEEDS VERIFICATION with MISA directly — licensing rules change and the sources are advisory firms with an interest in the answer.**) | 🔴 High. Cost, time, and ongoing obligations. |
| **Saudization (Nitaqat)** — only once you employ people locally | Reporting indicates a **30% localisation requirement for 46 engineering professions by 30 June 2026**, with Saudi employees needing a minimum salary of around **SAR 4,000/month** and contracts documented on Qiwa to count toward quota (**researched** — [motaded.com.sa](https://motaded.com.sa/blog/saudization); [enterprisehub.sa](https://enterprisehub.sa/blog/ksa-tech-company-setup-2026-strategic-guide/), accessed 2026-09-02. **NEEDS VERIFICATION.**) | 🔴 High **if** entity + local staff. ⚪ Not applicable to purely remote project work billed from Egypt. |
| **Arabic — and the right register** | Modern Standard Arabic and Gulf business norms, not Egyptian dialect. `[[Foundation Brief]]` §8 already sets this rule. | 🟢 Low. Code Square already writes both. |
| **English** | Standard for enterprise and multinational buyers. Code Square has English on the website (**stated**) and **none on social** (**stated**). | 🟡 Medium. A content gap, not a capability gap. |
| **Local presence / relationships** | Gulf B2B buying is relationship-led and reference-led. Remote-only vendors with no local reference are heavily discounted or ignored. | 🔴 High — the real barrier, more than any licence. |
| **Getting paid** | See §3.5. | 🟡 Medium. |
| **ZATCA e-invoicing** if invoicing as a Saudi entity | Saudi has its own mandated e-invoicing regime. **NEEDS VERIFICATION** of current phase requirements before invoicing locally. | 🟡 Medium |

### 3.4 The honest recommendation on the Gulf

> **Do not pursue Saudi Arabia as a market in the next 6–9 months.** Keep Jeddah and Riyadh listed as service areas, and take remote work if it arrives — but spend nothing to generate it.

Reasoning, consistent with `[[Beachhead Decision]]`:

1. Code Square has **no Gulf reference and no Gulf content**. Entering a relationship-led, reference-led market with neither is the weakest possible position.
2. The proof that would make Saudi winnable — a published maritime case study with a number — **does not exist yet in Egypt**.
3. Entity setup and Saudization costs are real; Egyptian delivery from Port Said costs nothing extra.
4. **The exception worth taking:** if the founder already has a personal contact in Jeddah or Riyadh, that is a warm channel, and warm channels beat market analysis every time. Take it opportunistically. It is still not a reason to build a Saudi campaign.

**The correct Gulf sequence:** win Port Said → publish a maritime case study with a number → produce the English version of it → *then* approach Gulf maritime firms with a portable, proven reference.

### 3.5 Currency and payment realities for an Egyptian vendor billing a Gulf client

Practical, and often the thing that actually kills a cross-border deal.

| Issue | Reality | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Which currency to price in** | Pricing in EGP transfers devaluation risk to Code Square. Pricing in USD or SAR transfers it to the client. | **Price Gulf work in USD or SAR.** Every EGP-priced foreign contract is a bet against your own currency. |
| **Receiving foreign currency in Egypt** | Egyptian FX regulations and bank practice around foreign-currency accounts and conversion have shifted repeatedly since 2023. | **TBD / NEEDS VERIFICATION with the company's own bank.** Do not act on general knowledge here — get it in writing from the bank before signing a foreign contract. |
| **Payment rails** | Bank transfer (SWIFT) is standard for B2B and is slow and fee-laden. Wise, Payoneer, and similar platforms are commonly used by Egyptian service exporters; **availability and limits are TBD** and change. Stripe is referenced on the Code Square site (**stated**) but Stripe's merchant availability in Egypt is **TBD — verify before promising it to any client.** | Confirm the actual rail before quoting. Never promise a payment method you have not tested. |
| **Deposits** | Recovery against a non-paying foreign client is impractical for an SME. | **50% up front, milestone-based thereafter, no exceptions on the first project.** Belongs in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]`. |
| **Contract and jurisdiction** | Cross-border contracts need a named governing law and dispute forum. | Get one contract template reviewed by an Egyptian lawyer with export experience. One-time cost, permanent benefit. **TBD.** |
| **Tax** | Export-of-services treatment, withholding tax, and double-taxation treaty positions all apply. | **TBD — accountant question, not a Claude question.** Do not guess in this vault. |

### 3.6 Pricing power: EGP vs SAR

| | Egypt (EGP) | Saudi (SAR) |
|---|---|---|
| Price level | Low. Anchored by cheap freelance and abundant agency supply (§1.2). | Higher. **Exact bands TBD.** |
| Currency risk to Code Square | High — earning in a currency that has devalued repeatedly | Low — SAR is USD-pegged |
| Cost to acquire a client | Low locally: drivable, referral-led, a public target list exists | High: travel, licensing, no reference, entrenched competition |
| Delivery cost | Same team, same cost | Same team, same cost |
| **Net margin per project** | Lower revenue, much lower acquisition cost | Higher revenue, much higher acquisition cost |
| **Verdict (proposed)** | **Better today.** Egypt is where Code Square can actually win a deal in the next two quarters. | **Better later** — after there is a portable, published, English-language reference. |
