# Brand Positioning — Code Square

## For future Claude

This note answers one question: **in the buyer's mind, what slot does Code Square occupy, and why can nobody else take it?** Read it before writing any headline, bio, ad, proposal, or sales script. It is downstream of [[Foundation Brief]] §3–§4 (which wins in any conflict) and upstream of [[Messaging Framework]], which turns this positioning into actual sentences.

> **Jargon defined once.** *Positioning* = the single idea you want to own in a buyer's head, stated so that a competitor cannot honestly say the same thing. *Point of difference* = a reason to choose you. *Point of parity* = a box you must tick just to be considered — it wins nothing, but missing it loses everything. *Frame of reference* = the set of options the buyer is actually comparing you against.

---

## 1. Positioning statement (proposed — founder must approve)

> **For** Egyptian and Gulf businesses that have outgrown spreadsheets and off-the-shelf tools but cannot afford or justify an in-house technology team,
> **Code Square is** the outsourced product and engineering team
> **that** designs and builds the operating system the business actually runs on — and then keeps running it alongside the client,
> **unlike** freelancers (who disappear after handover) and template shops (who ship the same product to everyone),
> **because** we start by understanding the business before writing a line of code, and we stay after launch.

**AR (proposed):** كود سكوير هو فريق المنتج والهندسة الخارجي للشركات اللي كبرت على الإكسل والبرامج الجاهزة. بنبني النظام اللي شغلك بيمشي عليه — وبنفضل شغالين عليه معاك.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Category we are NOT competing in (proposed)** | "Digital agency" / "web design company" / "software house" — commodity categories in Egypt, priced by the hour, chosen by portfolio screenshots. |
| **Category we claim (proposed)** | **Outsourced product & engineering team** — فريق المنتج والهندسة الخارجي لشركتك. |
| **Category line (AR, proposed)** | فريق المنتج والهندسة الخارجي لشركتك |
| **Category line (EN, proposed)** | Your outsourced product & engineering team |

The category matters more than the tagline. A buyer who files Code Square under "web design" compares it to a freelancer at 5,000 EGP. A buyer who files it under "our technology team" compares it to the cost of hiring one.

## 3. The "Outsourced CTO" frame (stated language, proposed as the lead)

**This language is already on the website**, differentiator #7, verbatim:

> **عقلية الشراكة** — تكامل عميق مع فريقك للعمل كذراع تقني استراتيجي **(Outsourced CTO)**.

Lead with it. Reasons (proposed):

1. **It is already true and already written.** Nothing is being invented — it is being promoted from footnote to headline.
2. **A freelancer cannot claim it.** A freelancer sells hours of building. A CTO-level relationship sells decisions: what to build, what not to build, what it should cost, what breaks at scale.
3. **A template shop cannot claim it.** A template shop has no architecture opinion to sell.
4. **It reframes the price conversation.** The comparison stops being "your quote vs the other quote" and becomes "your retainer vs the salary of one senior engineer."
5. **It matches the existing delivery model.** The website already sells post-launch maintenance and iteration; the philosophy already says "not just service providers."

**The buyer-facing phrasing (proposed):**
- **AR:** تكنولوجيا كاملة بدون ما توظف فريق. — Full technology capability without hiring a team.
- **EN:** A full technology team on the outside of your payroll.

⚠️ **Obligation this frame creates.** "Outsourced CTO" is a promise about *judgement and continuity*, not code volume. It is only honest if Code Square (a) does real discovery before quoting, (b) says no to bad requests, and (c) is reachable after launch. If those three are not operationally true, drop the frame rather than dilute it. See [[Core Values and Operating Principles]].

## 4. Competitive frame of reference

Who the buyer is really choosing between (proposed — no competitor has been researched by name; do not invent competitor names).

| Alternative | What the buyer likes about it | Where it fails them | Code Square's counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A freelancer** | Cheapest, fast to start, one person to talk to | Single point of failure, no architecture, vanishes after delivery, no documentation | Continuity + a team + post-launch ownership |
| **A template / "website" shop** | Cheap, visible portfolio, fast | Same product for every client; breaks the moment the business is unusual | "لا قوالب جاهزة هنا" — custom-built, stated on the site |
| **Off-the-shelf SaaS** | Instant, low monthly cost, supported | Forces the business to change its process to fit the tool; can't model unusual workflows | We build around your workflow, not the reverse |
| **Hiring in-house** | Full control, always available | Salary + recruitment risk + management overhead + one person can't cover design, backend, mobile, and ops | The Outsourced CTO frame, directly |
| **A large Cairo agency** | Credibility, scale, brand names | Expensive, slow, junior team assigned, client is a small account | Senior attention, local presence, domain fluency |
| **Doing nothing / spreadsheets** | Free, familiar | The real incumbent. Breaks silently as the business grows | The whole one-liner is aimed at exactly this buyer |

**Note (proposed):** "doing nothing" is the most common competitor and the hardest. Most messaging should be aimed at making the cost of the spreadsheet visible, not at beating a named vendor.

## 6. The four unfair advantages, expanded

From [[Foundation Brief]] §3. An *unfair advantage* = something a competitor cannot replicate this year even if they read this document.

### 6.1 Osama Sakr Manning Agency — the maritime asset

- **Evidence (stated):** website portfolio item #4 — *منصة لإدارة الوكالات تشمل التوظيف، تتبع المشاريع، ولوحات تحليلات لحظية لمراقبة الأداء.* Labelled `نظام B2B`, external client.
- **Why it is unfair:** a *manning agency* is a maritime crew recruitment business. Port Said sits on the Suez Canal. Within driving distance there is a dense cluster of shipping agencies, crewing agents, freight forwarders, customs brokers, and ship chandlers — high-budget, workflow-heavy, and almost entirely unserved by local software vendors who speak their language, both literally and operationally. Code Square has already shipped one and can say so.
- **What must happen to activate it (proposed):** written publication permission from the client, plus at least one number (placements processed, hours saved per week, errors eliminated). Until then this advantage exists but is unusable in public.
- **Risk:** a single reference is a single reference. Do not let it drift into implying a maritime practice with many clients.

### 6.2 Techno Square + Online Techno Square — the education asset

- **Evidence (stated):** portfolio items #2 and the Facebook case study — education platform, integrated mobile app, dashboard, enrolment form wired directly into the Techno Square dashboard, mascot **تاتا 🤖** used as a journey companion.
- **Why it is unfair:** it is a **live, demo-able, permission-free** proof. Every academy, training centre, and private teacher can be shown a working product in the first meeting rather than a slide.
- **Trade-off:** lower ticket size than maritime. Run it as the volume and demo engine, not as the lead position.

### 6.3 The M Tech Square group — the case-study factory

- **Evidence (stated):** six sibling entities, group site built by Code Square, group strapline "BEYOND SQUARES".
- **Why it is unfair:** most agencies at 74 followers have nothing to publish because clients say no. Code Square has a permanent internal client that always says yes. It can ship, publish, and iterate real product work continuously.
- **Risk to manage:** if every case study is internal, buyers discount them. Always pair internal work with the two external references.

### 6.4 A pharmacist founder — the healthcare asset

- **Evidence (stated):** founder is a pharmacist by training. That is the whole of the evidence — the name, the bio, and any clinical experience are **TBD**.
- **Why it is unfair:** it converts "we build software" into "we understand your dispensing workflow, your stock expiry problem, your insurance claim cycle." A competitor can hire a developer; it cannot hire a decade of pharmacy fluency.
- **What must happen to activate it (proposed):** one healthcare pilot from the founder's own network, traded for a documented before/after. Until then this is a *credibility asset in conversation*, not a published claim. Never write "we specialise in healthcare software" with zero healthcare projects delivered.

## 8. What Code Square explicitly refuses to be (proposed)

A position is defined as much by refusals as by claims. Each refusal below is directly traceable to something the company already published.

| We are not | Traceable to | What that means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **A template shop** | "لا قوالب جاهزة هنا" · "Custom-built. مش Copy & Paste." | We decline work whose only requirement is "make it like this other site". |
| **A trend chaser** | "لا نطارد الصيحات العابرة" | We do not add AI, blockchain, or the framework of the month because it is fashionable. |
| **A code vendor paid by the feature** | "لسنا مجرد مبرمجين" | We will argue a feature out of scope if the user does not need it. |
| **A hand-off-and-vanish supplier** | "مش بنسلم ونمشي... إحنا مكملين معاك" | Every engagement has a named post-launch arrangement or we say why it does not. |
| **The cheapest option** | `$$$` price signal + Outsourced CTO frame | We do not win on price and should not bid where price is the only criterion. |
| **A generalist for "anyone with an idea"** | The 74-follower result of doing exactly that | We choose a segment and turn down poor fits. |
| **A company that claims what it cannot prove** | [[Foundation Brief]] §0 rule 5 | No claim ships without a named artifact behind it. See the banned list in [[Messaging Framework]]. |
