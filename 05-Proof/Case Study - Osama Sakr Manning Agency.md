## 1. Client and context (stated)

**Osama Sakr Manning Agency** — an external, B2B client. The website describes the delivered system as `منصة لإدارة الوكالات تشمل التوظيف، تتبع المشاريع، ولوحات تحليلات لحظية لمراقبة الأداء` / "Agency management platform for recruitment, tracking, and team collaboration."

*Jargon note for the founder: a **manning agency** (also called a crewing agency) recruits, vets, documents, and places seafarers — officers, engineers, ratings — onto ships on behalf of shipowners and ship managers. The work is heavy on documents (certificates, medicals, visas, seaman's books), heavy on deadlines (a crew change happens on the day the vessel is in port, not a day later), and heavy on compliance.*

`❓ NEEDS FROM FOUNDER:` Required before anything is written:
- Is the client based in Port Said? Do they serve Suez Canal transits, or a wider fleet?
- Roughly how many seafarers are on their books, and how many placements per month?
- Who do they place for — shipowners, ship managers, or both?
- Named contact, role, and best channel
- Is the relationship live (support/retainer) or delivered and closed?
- Are they on good enough terms to give a testimonial **and take a reference call from a prospect?** *(A reference call from one manning agency to another is worth more than any case study.)*

## 4. Our approach — mapped to the four-phase methodology

**التأسيس — Discovery.** `❓ NEEDS FROM FOUNDER:` How did this engagement begin, and how did Code Square learn the crewing workflow? **The answer to this is a marketing asset in itself** — "we sat in a manning agency and learned how a crew change actually works" is exactly the sentence that opens doors at the next shipping company, and it is unfakeable by a competitor.

## 9. Why this project is the company's strongest strategic asset (proposed)

This section is not for publication — it is the reasoning behind why this case study gets founder time before anything else.

**The geography is the advantage.** Code Square is in **Port Said**, at the northern mouth of the **Suez Canal**. Around it sits a dense, capital-rich, and software-starved cluster: shipping agencies, crewing and manning agents, freight forwarders, customs brokers, bunkering suppliers, ship chandlers, surveyors, and stevedoring companies. These businesses run on documents, deadlines, and coordination — the exact shape of problem that custom software solves best — and they are almost entirely served today by spreadsheets, WhatsApp, and paper.

**The barrier to entry is domain language, not code.** Any competent agency in Cairo can build a recruitment platform. Almost none of them can sit with a crewing manager and understand why a certificate expiry date matters more than a candidate's CV, or why a crew change window is measured in hours. Code Square has already done that work once, with a paying client. **That is the moat.** A generic Port Said agency cannot follow, and a Cairo agency will not bother.

**Why it beats the other beachheads:** it is the highest-ticket segment available, it is on the company's doorstep so meetings cost a taxi ride rather than a flight, and it is the only segment where a named local reference already exists. `[[Foundation Brief]]` §4 recommends leading with it, running education (Techno Square) in parallel as the volume engine.

**What it unlocks, concretely:**
- An opening line for every maritime conversation: "بنينا نظام لوكالة تطقيم في بورسعيد" — a sentence no competitor in the city can say
- A reference call between two manning agencies who likely already know each other
- A second and third product built from the same spine: crew document and certificate tracking, vessel/port-call coordination, customs clearance workflow
- A defensible reason to be the vendor of record for an entire vertical in one city

**Therefore:** getting permission and a number from this client is the single highest-value action available. Do it before spending anything on ads, content, or design.
