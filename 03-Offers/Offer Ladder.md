# Offer Ladder — Code Square

## For future Claude

`[[Foundation Brief]]` §2 Layer 4 states the core problem this file solves: **"A follower's only action is to message and hope. There is no named, priced, bookable first step."** §6 proposes three rungs. This file makes those three rungs real enough to execute on Monday.

Rules for editing:
- The three rungs come from `[[Foundation Brief]]` §6 and are `proposed`. Their *names, promises, and structure* are specified here. Their **prices are not** — every price is `TBD — founder must set`, with the decision framework in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]`.
- Rung 1 (the free Diagnostic Session) is the **only CTA that should appear on social media** (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §6.1). If a future note proposes a second social CTA, it contradicts the brief.
- The agenda, question set, and one-page output template in §4 are meant to be used verbatim. They are the actual work product.
- Do not add a fourth rung. Three is the design.

## 1. Why a ladder at all — for the founder

A ladder solves one problem: **a stranger will not hand a small company in Port Said a large sum of money for a system they cannot yet picture.**

The ask goes up as trust goes up:

| Rung | The client risks | The client learns | Code Square learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Diagnostic | 45 minutes | Whether these people understand my business | Whether this is a real buyer |
| 2 — Blueprint | A small, fixed, credited fee | Exactly what will be built, what it costs, and how long | The true scope — so the build quote is defensible |
| 3 — Build & Run | The real budget | Whether the system works | Whether this is a long-term account |

Two structural properties make this work:

1. **Rung 2 gets money on day one.** A prospect who has paid anything is a fundamentally different person from one who has not. And the fee is **credited against the build** (§5.6), so the buyer loses nothing by starting.
2. **Rung 2 protects the build quote.** Quoting a fixed-price build off a 45-minute call is the single most reliable way for an agency to lose money (`[[Scoping and Estimation Guide]]` trap #1). The Blueprint is the paid mechanism that makes rung 3 safe to price.

## 3. Rung 1 — جلسة تشخيص / Diagnostic Session

### Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name (AR) | **جلسة تشخيص** |
| Name (EN) | **Diagnostic Session** |
| Price | **Free**, and say the word free |
| Duration | 30–45 minutes. Hard stop at 45. |
| Format | Video call, phone, WhatsApp call, or in person in Port Said |
| Language | Egyptian dialect by default; MSA or English for Gulf and formal prospects |
| Delivered by | **The founder personally.** Not delegated. This rung *is* the pharmacist-founder advantage (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §3). |
| Booking | A real booking link with real calendar availability. `TBD` — tool not chosen; tracked in `[[Open Questions and Decisions Needed]]` |

### The promise (use this wording)

**AR:** خلال ٤٥ دقيقة هنفهم شغلك، ونحدد فين بالظبط بيضيع وقتك وفلوسك دلوقتي، وتخرج بورقة مكتوبة فيها التشخيص وأول خطوة عملية — تاخدها معاك سواء اشتغلنا مع بعض أو لأ.

**EN:** In 45 minutes we map how your business actually runs, identify exactly where time and money are leaking today, and you leave with a written one-page diagnosis and a recommended first step — yours to keep whether or not we work together.

**Why "yours to keep whether or not we work together" is load-bearing:** it removes the fear that this is a disguised sales call, and it is the only reason a busy shipping-agency owner gives 45 minutes to a company with 74 followers. It must be true. Deliver the page even when the answer is "you do not need software."

### Qualification criteria — who gets offered a Diagnostic

Deliberately generous. This rung is the top of the funnel; the qualifying happens *during* it.

| Offer it to | Do not offer it to |
|---|---|
| Any business owner or manager with an operational problem | Someone asking only "how much for a website?" with no context — send the `[[Service Catalog]]` and a qualifying question first |
| Anyone in beachhead segments A (maritime/logistics), B (education), C (healthcare) | Students, job seekers, other agencies looking for subcontracting |
| An existing client with a new problem | A prospect who has already had a Diagnostic and taken no step — offer a written follow-up instead, not a second free hour |
| Warm referrals from the founder's network | Anyone who cannot name a decision-maker |

**One free Diagnostic per company.** A second free session is consulting given away.

### The agenda — 45 minutes, run it in this order

| Min | Block | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Frame** | "I'll ask about how your business runs today. I'm not going to pitch. At the end you get a written page. If software isn't the answer I'll tell you that." | Sets the contract, kills defensiveness, makes the page credible |
| 3–15 | **The business** | What the business does, who its customers are, what a typical job/transaction looks like end to end | You cannot diagnose an operation you cannot describe |
| 15–28 | **The pain** | Where it breaks. What is manual, re-typed, lost, chased, late. Who complains | This is the paid product; the rest is context |
| 28–35 | **The numbers** | How often, how long, how much it costs when it goes wrong | **Never skip.** Without a number there is no case for spending, and no case study later (`[[Foundation Brief]]` §7.2) |
| 35–40 | **The reality check** | Budget range, timeline pressure, who decides, what else they're considering, what happens if they do nothing | Qualification. Do it openly, not sneakily |
| 40–45 | **The close** | Say the diagnosis out loud, name the first step, state when the written page arrives, name the Blueprint if it fits | An unnamed next step is no next step |

**Run to time.** A Diagnostic that runs 90 minutes has stopped being a diagnostic and started being free consulting.

### The question set — ask these, in this order

**A. The business (3–15 min)**
1. لو حد سألك، بتشتغلوا إيه بالظبط؟ إيه اللي بيدفع عميلك فلوس عشانه؟
2. خدني في رحلة شغلانة واحدة من أولها لآخرها — من أول ما العميل يتواصل لحد ما الفلوس تدخل.
3. مين بيمسك كل خطوة في الرحلة دي؟ كام واحد بيلمسها؟
4. بتستخدموا إيه دلوقتي؟ إكسيل، واتساب، دفاتر، برنامج جاهز؟
5. البيانات بتتكتب كام مرة في أكتر من مكان؟ *(the re-typing count is the single strongest automation signal)*

**B. The pain (15–28 min)**
6. إيه الحاجة اللي لو اتصلحت بكرة الصبح هتفرق معاك أكتر حاجة؟
7. آخر مرة حصلت مشكلة كبيرة كانت إيه؟ وإيه اللي سببها؟
8. إيه اللي بيتعمل بالإيد وحاسس إنه المفروض يتعمل لوحده؟
9. عملاؤك بيشتكوا من إيه؟
10. إيه الحاجة اللي مش قادر تشوفها دلوقتي وعايز تشوفها؟ *(surfaces the reporting/dashboard need)*
11. جربت تحل ده قبل كده؟ حصل إيه؟ *(a failed previous vendor changes everything about how you sell and price)*

**C. The numbers (28–35 min)**
12. الخطوة دي بتاخد قد إيه في اليوم/الأسبوع؟ ومين اللي بيعملها؟
13. بتحصل كام مرة في الشهر؟
14. لما تغلط، بتكلف كام — فلوس، وقت، أو عميل؟
15. لو الوقت ده رجعلك، هتعمل بيه إيه؟ *(converts saved time into money — critical for the value-based ceiling in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §3)*

**D. The reality check (35–40 min)**
16. مين اللي بيقول آه أو لأ على حاجة زي دي غيرك؟
17. حطيت في بالك ميزانية للموضوع ده؟ حتى تقريبًا؟ *(ask it plainly; hedging teaches them to hedge)*
18. إيه اللي بيخليها مستعجلة دلوقتي؟ في تاريخ معين؟
19. بتفكر في حلول تانية؟ برنامج جاهز، حد تاني، توظيف؟
20. لو مانعملش حاجة السنة دي، هيحصل إيه؟

**E. The close (40–45 min)**
21. "اللي فهمته إن المشكلة الأساسية هي ___. صح ولا فايتني حاجة؟" *(always play the diagnosis back and let them correct it)*
22. "أول خطوة اللي أنصح بيها هي ___."
23. "هبعتلك الورقة خلال ٢٤ ساعة." *(and actually send it — the site already promises a 24-hour response nobody owns; `[[Lead Intake and CRM]]`)*

## 4. Rung 2 — خريطة المشروع / Project Blueprint

### Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name (AR) | **خريطة المشروع** |
| Name (EN) | **Project Blueprint** |
| Price | **Fixed.** `TBD — founder must set.` Framework in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §5 |
| Duration | 1–2 weeks |
| Payment | **100% up front.** It is small, fixed, and fully credited — there is no reason to invoice it in halves |
| Delivered by | Founder + one designer and/or one engineer |
| Credited | **Yes — 100% against the build**, if the build starts within 60 days. See `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §5 |

### The promise

**AR:** قبل ما تدفع تكلفة التنفيذ كاملة، بنقعد أسبوع أو اتنين نحوّل الفكرة لخطة مكتوبة: إيه اللي هيتبني بالظبط، بأي ترتيب، في قد إيه من الوقت، وبكام — سعر ثابت. الخطة دي ملكك، وتقدر تنفذها معانا أو مع أي حد تاني. ولو كملت معانا، تكلفة الخريطة بتتخصم بالكامل.

**EN:** Before you commit a build budget, we spend one to two weeks turning the idea into a written plan: exactly what gets built, in what order, in how long, and for how much — as a fixed quote. The plan is yours, and you may execute it with us or with anyone else. If you build with us, the Blueprint fee is credited in full.

**"You may execute it with anyone else" is not a giveaway.** It is what makes a stranger comfortable paying. In practice a client who has spent one to two weeks in a room with the team rarely takes the document elsewhere — and if they do, the fee covered the work and Code Square lost nothing but a project it was going to lose anyway.

### Qualification criteria — who is offered a Blueprint

**All five must be true.** This is a real gate, not a formality.

| # | Criterion | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A **specific operational problem** was named in the Diagnostic — not "we want an app" | Vague brief in, vague blueprint out |
| 2 | The **decision-maker** was in the Diagnostic or is confirmed reachable | Blueprinting for someone who cannot buy is unpaid research |
| 3 | A **budget range** exists and overlaps Code Square's floor (`[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §2) | A Blueprint for a project that can never be afforded wastes two weeks |
| 4 | A **timeline reason** exists — a season, a contract, a growth pressure | No urgency means indefinite delay |
| 5 | The problem sits inside the `[[Service Catalog]]` | Do not blueprint work Code Square cannot deliver |

Fails 3 or 4? Offer a smaller paid step instead (a UI/UX prototype sprint, or an integration feasibility week — `[[Service Catalog]]` §6, §7). Fails 1 or 2? Another Diagnostic with the right person. Fails 5? Refer it out; referrals come back.

### What the client receives — named deliverables

| # | Deliverable | Plain language |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **وثيقة فهم المشكلة** / Problem Definition | The problem, the current process, and the cost of leaving it alone — in the client's own words, agreed in writing before anything else |
| 2 | **رحلة المستخدم** / User Journey Map | Every person who touches the process, what they do, where it breaks today, and how it works after |
| 3 | **قائمة المميزات بالأولوية** / Prioritised Feature List | Every feature, sorted must / should / could / not now, **with the reason for each ranking**. The "not now" column is the most important one on the page |
| 4 | **النطاق على مراحل** / Phased Scope | What is in V1, V2, V3. V1 is deliberately, uncomfortably small |
| 5 | **المخطط المعماري** / Architecture Outline | One diagram plus one page: the pieces, the integrations, the data, the risks. This is where "مهندسو أنظمة" is proven rather than claimed |
| 6 | **الشاشات الرئيسية** / Key Screen Wireframes | 5–10 grey-box screens so the client can *see* it. Not a full design |
| 7 | **الجدول الزمني** / Timeline | Phases, milestones, and the client's own review deadlines marked in |
| 8 | **عرض السعر الثابت** / Fixed Build Quote | A real, signable, fixed-price proposal for V1 — valid 30 days. This is the point of the whole exercise |
| 9 | **سجل المخاطر** / Risk Register | What could go wrong, how likely, and what it would do to time and cost. Named in advance, it is engineering; named after, it is an excuse |
| 10 | **التوصية** / The Recommendation | Build it, phase it, buy something off the shelf instead, or do nothing. **Honestly.** |

Deliverable 10 must be capable of saying "do not build this." If it never says that, the Blueprint is a sales document and buyers will eventually notice.

### How the Blueprint is run — 2 weeks

| Day | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kick-off workshop (2–3h) with all stakeholders | Problem statement draft |
| 2–3 | Interviews with the people who do the work daily | Journey map draft |
| 4 | Review current systems, data, and integration surfaces | Constraints list |
| 5 | **Checkpoint 1** — play back the problem and journey. Client corrects | Signed problem definition |
| 6–7 | Feature prioritisation workshop + phasing | Feature list, V1 scope |
| 8–9 | Architecture and wireframes | Diagram, 5–10 screens |
| 10 | Estimation using `[[Scoping and Estimation Guide]]` | Internal estimate |
| 11 | Risk register + recommendation | |
| 12 | **Checkpoint 2** — present the pack and the fixed quote live, never by email | Signed acceptance |

Two checkpoints, not one. A Blueprint the client first sees on the last day is a Blueprint they can reject wholesale.

### Maps to the methodology

The Blueprint **is** phase 1 **التأسيس** and part of phase 2 **المعمارية** of the website's 4-phase methodology (`[[2026-09-02 - Website Evidence (mtechsquare.com-code-square)]]`), sold separately. Sell it in exactly those words — it proves the published methodology is real practice and not website decoration.

> **Methodology conflict, unresolved.** The site publishes 4 phases (التأسيس / المعمارية / الهندسة / الإطلاق والتطوير); Facebook publishes 5 stages. `[[Foundation Brief]]` §0.6 requires picking one. **This vault uses the website's 4-phase model throughout.** Founder must confirm and retire the other. `[[Open Questions and Decisions Needed]]`.

### Natural next step

Rung 3, quoted inside deliverable 8. Present it live at Checkpoint 2, with the credit stated explicitly: "the Blueprint fee comes off this number."

### Conversion metrics

| Metric | Target `proposed` |
|---|---|
| **Blueprint → Build** | **60–70%.** Below 50% means the qualification gate is leaking |
| Blueprint delivered on time | 100% — it is 10 days, there is no excuse |
| Build quote accepted without renegotiation | > 70% |
| Blueprint fee actually credited | Should equal the build conversion rate; a gap means the credit is not being honoured or not being communicated |
| Estimate accuracy | Actual build days ÷ Blueprint estimate within ±20%. **Track this on every project** — it is the only way `[[Scoping and Estimation Guide]]` improves |

## 5. Rung 3 — البناء والتشغيل / Build & Run

### Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name (AR) | **البناء والتشغيل** |
| Name (EN) | **Build & Run** |
| Price | `TBD — founder must set.` Framework in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` |

### The promise

**AR:** بنبني النسخة الأولى بنطاق وسعر ثابتين متفق عليهم من الخريطة، وبعد التسليم بنفضل معاك بعقد شهري: صيانة، دعم، وتطوير مستمر. مش بنسلم ونمشي.

**EN:** We build V1 to the fixed scope and fixed price agreed in the Blueprint, then stay with you on a monthly agreement: maintenance, support, and continuous improvement.

The Arabic line **"مش بنسلم ونمشي"** is Code Square's own published words (`[[2026-09-02 - Facebook Page Evidence]]`, Service 7). Use it verbatim.

### Qualification criteria

| # | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 1 | A Blueprint exists — **or** an equivalent paid discovery. See the exception below |
| 2 | V1 scope is written, phased, and signed |
| 3 | Deposit terms accepted (`[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §7) |
| 4 | A named client decision-maker committed to the 3-working-day feedback SLA |
| 5 | Third-party access and accounts confirmed available |
| 6 | The retainer conversation has already happened — **before** the build starts, not after |

> **The no-Blueprint exception.** Occasionally a client will only buy the whole thing at once. That is fine — but then the *first phase of the build is a paid discovery phase* with a **re-quote gate** at its end, where either party may stop and the client owes only that phase. Never fixed-price a whole build off a 45-minute conversation. `[[Scoping and Estimation Guide]]` trap #1.

### What the client receives

Everything in the relevant `[[Service Catalog]]` entry, plus:

| Deliverable | Note |
|---|---|
| Working V1 in production | Against signed acceptance criteria |
| Source code and all accounts | In the client's name, on final payment |
| Handover documentation and runbook | How it works, how to operate it, what to do when it breaks |
| One training session | Recorded, so new staff can be trained without a new invoice |
| 30-day defect warranty | |
| **Monthly retainer** | Tiers in `[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §6 |
| Quarterly technology review | Where "Outsourced CTO" is actually delivered |
| **Documented before/after numbers** | A contractual deliverable, not a favour (`[[Service Catalog]]` §9) |

### Delivery cadence — non-negotiable rhythm

| Rhythm | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Written progress note: done / next / blocked / decisions needed | Silence is what clients interpret as trouble |
| Every 2 weeks | Live demo of working software | The only honest measure of progress. A percentage is an opinion; a demo is a fact |
| At each milestone | Written acceptance sign-off | Ties payment to a signature, not to a mood |
| Monthly (retainer) | Report: incidents, fixes, hours used, recommendations | |
| Quarterly (retainer) | Technology review with the owner | Where the next project is discovered |

### Natural next step

V2 → V3 → more of the business brought onto the system. **The retainer is not the end of the ladder — it is the surface the next project is sold from.** The quarterly review is where an existing client becomes the source of the highest-margin work in the company, at zero acquisition cost.

### Conversion metrics

| Metric | Target `proposed` |
|---|---|
| **Build → retainer attach** | **80%.** The single most important number in the company |
| Retainer 12-month survival | > 70% |
| Client → second project within 12 months | > 50% |
| On-time V1 delivery | > 80% |
| Actual vs estimated days | ±20% |
| Change requests **billed** vs absorbed | > 80% billed. Absorbed scope is invisible margin loss (`[[Pricing and Packaging]]` §8) |
| Referral rate | ≥ 1 warm referral per satisfied client per year — ask at the quarterly review, explicitly |

## 6. The full path, end to end

```
Social post / website / referral
        ↓  (one CTA, always the same)
   جلسة تشخيص — Free, 45 min
        ↓  one-page diagnosis within 24h  ·  target 25–35% →
   خريطة المشروع — Fixed price, 1–2 weeks, 100% credited
        ↓  fixed build quote at Checkpoint 2  ·  target 60–70% →
   البناء والتشغيل — V1 fixed scope
        ↓  retainer line was in the original proposal  ·  target 80% →
   الاحتفاظ الشهري — Monthly retainer
        ↓  quarterly technology review →
   V2 / V3 / next department  →  referral  →  back to the top
```

**Compound arithmetic (illustrative, not a forecast):** 20 Diagnostics → 6 Blueprints (30%) → 4 builds (65%) → 3 retainers (80%). Three recurring accounts and four projects from twenty conversations. The leverage point is not the close rate at the bottom — **it is the number of Diagnostics at the top**, which is currently constrained by distribution, which is `[[Foundation Brief]]` §9 Priority 0–2. No pricing work fixes that.

## 7. What is deliberately NOT on this ladder

| Not offered | Why |
|---|---|
| A free proposal or free quote for a large build | That is the Blueprint, and it is paid. Free quotes are how agencies do a week of unpaid analysis and lose |
| A second free Diagnostic | Free consulting with extra steps |
| Hourly freelance work | No margin, no relationship, no positioning. Contradicts "Outsourced CTO" |
| A cheap template website tier | Competes on price against people with no overheads, and destroys the systems-engineering position |
| A fourth "enterprise" rung | Nothing to put in it yet. Add when a real enterprise client exists |
